What happens between your question and the evidence.

Architecture

LexTutor is not a language model sitting on top of a document base. It is a multi-stage legal pipeline, in which generation begins only after the sources have been assembled, governed and verified. This page describes the five layers of that pipeline — in the order your question passes through them.

Verification

The question is analysed the way a lawyer would analyse it: the areas of law involved, the applicable codes, the language, the canton. The system distinguishes what the letters alone confuse — Art. 368 CO governs defects in the work; Art. 368 CC concerns adult protection. A short question and a three-page statement of facts do not follow the same path: the depth of analysis adjusts to the complexity of the request.

Several retrieval paths converge — semantic, lexical, terminological, and through the citation graph — across the federal and cantonal corpora. The terminological path draws on the trilingual legal thesaurus of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court: a concept phrased in French finds its German and Italian equivalents. The graph path follows the links between provisions and decisions, and returns sources that a keyword search never sees.

The provisions critical to the area of law are guaranteed a place in the file; noise is set aside; the hierarchy of norms is respected. The file remains deliberately bounded: a controlled number of sources, actually read, is worth more than an accumulation nobody controls. It is this layer — not the model — that decides what enters your file.

The model writes under constraint: every statement must rest on a source in the file — never the other way round. It is not permitted to fill in from its training memory what the sources do not say. If the file does not settle a point, the analysis says so explicitly.

Before anything is displayed, every citation is validated against the graph: the provision exists, the decision exists, the reference is exact. What cannot be verified is flagged as such — neither hidden nor rephrased. Every source in the ledger carries its link to the official publication — Fedlex, the Swiss Federal Supreme Court’s website or the cantonal compilation: the final check is yours to make.

The authority graph.

ON REQUEST more than a million links between provisions and decisions. The graph is not decoration: it feeds your file with sources a conventional search misses.

Three languages, one body of law.

One canonical record per enactment — the CO, not parallel corpora for OR and CO. Your questions in French, German or Italian converge on the same sources, with no loss and no divergence.

Modes isolated by design.

Research, Pro and JurisExpress are separate paths: what serves rapid triage never degrades a defensible analysis, and vice versa.

Technical questions.

Which sources are covered?

Federal law in force — codes, Federal Acts, ordinances — and the case law of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, complemented by cantonal corpora under expansion. Detailed coverage: ON REQUEST codes and acts, ON REQUEST indexed decisions, cantons covered ON REQUEST. Legal scholarship is not included at this stage.

How is the corpus kept up to date?

Fedlex and the official cantonal compilations are the sole references for the state of the law. Every source in the ledger shows its status in force at the date of the analysis; a repealed or revised provision is flagged as such. Synchronisation cadence: ON REQUEST.

Which languages are supported?

French, German and Italian, on one canonical record per enactment: StPO and CPP designate one and the same Criminal Procedure Code, not three corpora. Legal terminology is aligned across the three languages through the trilingual thesaurus of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court.

What are the acknowledged limits?

LexTutor covers neither legal scholarship nor all twenty-six cantons at this stage. It does not replace the lawyer’s judgement: it prepares the evidence file, it does not plead. Above all, it states what it does not know — a point the sources cannot settle is reported as unsettled, not papered over. A tool that knows its limits is a tool you can rely on before a court.

How is this different from a general-purpose chatbot?

A general-purpose chatbot generates first and cites sometimes. In LexTutor, generation begins only after the sources have been assembled, governed and verified: the model writes from a governed file, under a citation constraint, and every reference is validated before display. Not an answer. An evidence file.

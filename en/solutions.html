Law firms

etudes

Every statement. Its source.

Research absorbs hours no one bills, and the risk remains undiminished: an imprecise citation will be used against you in court. Checking every reference by hand, submission after submission, is no longer tenable.

Typical use

A contractor client disputes a notice of defects raised against him as having come too late. The lawyer describes the facts; LexTutor traces them to Articles 367 and 368 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO) — inspection of the work, notice of defects, rights of the customer — and presents the relevant Federal Supreme Court case law, ranked by authority. The lawyer checks every source on Fedlex and on the Federal Supreme Court website before taking a position.

The LexTutor proof

The Source Ledger accompanies every analysis: exact article, exact decision, normative rank, status in force, official link. The authority graph shows how the sources cite one another — including the related provisions a keyword search will not surface.

What the opposing party will check, you have already checked.

Public prosecution offices

ministeres-publics

Every statement. Its source.

Voluminous case files, coercive measures to substantiate under deadline, documents moving between French, German and Italian. A legal basis cited from memory is not enough: the defence will check every reference.

Typical use

A prosecutor prepares a request for surveillance measures: first the conditions of Article 269 of the Swiss Criminal Procedure Code (CPP) and the catalogue offences, then the Federal Supreme Court case law on proportionality. For the international side of the case, the prosecutor moves to the framework of the Federal Act on International Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters (EIMP) — without changing tools, in the language of the proceedings.

The LexTutor proof

One canonical version per act, searchable in French, German and Italian: StPO and CPP designate the same text, the same articles, the same official links. The graph connects the Criminal Code, criminal procedure and the case law; the Source Ledger records the status in force of every provision cited.

In-house legal teams

directions-juridiques

Every statement. Its source.

Questions arrive from across the company, urgent and heterogeneous: a contested dismissal, a data clause, a supplier dispute. You must answer quickly, without outsourcing everything — and stand behind every answer.

Typical use

Human resources asks whether a termination with immediate effect is defensible. In-house counsel verifies the good cause requirement of Article 337 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO) and the Federal Supreme Court case law that defines its contours, then delivers a written position with the sources attached — or decides to escalate to external counsel, with the file already built.

The LexTutor proof

The Source Ledger turns an internal opinion into a documented position: every statement carries its reference, verifiable in one click against the official source. For groups operating across Switzerland’s language regions, the same question can be handled in French, German or Italian, on the same canonical body of law.

Cantonal administrations

administrations

Every statement. Its source.

Cantonal law lives in its own compilation, interlocking with higher-ranking federal law, and generic tools ignore it. Every decision must cite its exact legal basis — any imprecision opens the door to an appeal.

Typical use

A lawyer in a cantonal department drafts a decision open to appeal. They start from the applicable cantonal act, verify its implementing ordinances, check how it interacts with higher-ranking federal law, and identify the procedural provisions the decision must address. All of it cited, dated, verifiable.

The LexTutor proof

LexTutor covers federal and cantonal law in a single graph: ON REQUEST. The Source Ledger distinguishes the normative rank of every source — constitution, act, ordinance, federal or cantonal — and flags its status in force.
